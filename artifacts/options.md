# Options — Family Cloud fictional case

All options below are analytical choices for a fictional scenario, not
decisions about a real operator or product.

## Option A — Keep as is

- **Approach:** make no product or campaign changes while validating event
  definitions, eligible-trial counts, and offer exposure.
- **Potential value:** avoids spending against a possibly synthetic or
  measurement-driven decline and creates a trustworthy baseline.
- **Strongest objection:** if real users are confused at a fixable point, delay
  continues avoidable conversion loss.
- **Disqualifying evidence:** verified tracking plus real, repeated evidence
  of a specific, addressable barrier and a safe intervention with material
  expected value.
- **Risks/costs:** opportunity cost; analysis time; no immediate experience
  improvement.

## Option B — Close the direction

- **Approach:** stop new acquisition and feature investment, preserve existing
  commitments, and plan a safe wind-down or migration if the product were real.
- **Potential value:** limits continued spend if validated incremental lifetime
  value is persistently below fully loaded acquisition and service costs.
- **Strongest objection:** the scenario contains no evidence of real demand,
  cohort lifetime, cannibalization, or customer transition costs; closure is
  irreversible and could destroy option value.
- **Disqualifying evidence:** positive incremental contribution, retention or
  ecosystem value, or customer commitments that exceed the measured costs of
  continuing.
- **Risks/costs:** churn, refunds, migration/support burden, brand damage, and
  loss of future learning.

## Option C — Clarify onboarding and family-sharing permissions

- **Approach:** in a controlled test, explain the family-sharing boundary and
  storage value before trial signup; keep price and eligibility unchanged.
- **Potential value:** tests the fictional feedback hypothesis about sharing
  clarity while isolating one product-comprehension intervention.
- **Strongest objection:** snippets are invented; the assumed confusion may not
  exist or may not affect willingness to pay. Additional copy can increase
  friction.
- **Disqualifying evidence:** no change in comprehension, activation, or
  paid-conversion relative to control, or meaningful increases in abandonment,
  complaints, or privacy misunderstanding.
- **Risks/costs:** engineering/experiment cost, added funnel friction, and
  false attribution if exposure is not randomized.

## Option D — Make trial terms and paid value explicit

- **Approach:** clearly disclose actual trial duration, renewal date, price,
  cancellation path, storage value, and service limitations before
  confirmation; pair the disclosure with a simple post-trial value summary.
- **Potential value:** tests whether transparent terms and a concrete benefit
  address the separate price/renewal uncertainty hypothesis.
- **Strongest objection:** conversion could fall when price becomes salient;
  bundling disclosure and benefit makes it difficult to identify which element
  caused an effect.
- **Disqualifying evidence:** comprehension improves but paid conversion and
  paid retention do not, or conversion gains are offset by refunds, complaints,
  margin loss, or cannibalization.
- **Risks/costs:** legal/content review, billing accuracy requirements, and
  increased initial abandonment.

## Comparison and shortlist

The demo has no real-user evidence to estimate option impact or rank expected
conversion. Option A is the safe immediate posture; Option C is the proposed
first *test*, not a production rollout. Option D is a distinct follow-up test
after price, duration, and cancellation terms are verified. Option B remains
available if real incremental economics are negative after a validated
measurement period.
